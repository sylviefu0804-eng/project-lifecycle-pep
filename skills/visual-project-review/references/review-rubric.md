# Visual review rubric

Apply only the sections relevant to the deliverable. A PASS means the rendered or exported result was actually inspected at an appropriate scale.

## 1. Context and baseline

- Project, scope, audience, medium, and page or zone role are explicit.
- The active Baseline and Approved Seeds belong to this project and scope.
- Seed IDs or source paths are recorded; no Candidate is represented as Approved.
- Rejected directions are visible as constraints and are not silently revived.
- In a mature project, changes are derived from the Baseline rather than rebuilt from memory.

## 2. Structure

- Each page or zone has one primary job.
- The sequence supports the intended narrative and decision.
- Primary, secondary, and supporting information are distinguishable.
- Content volume fits the physical or screen context.
- Repeated content has a reason; missing context is supplied.
- Calls to action, labels, legends, dates, units, and navigation are unambiguous.

## 3. Copy

- Headline, subhead, body, caption, and metadata roles are consistent.
- Copy is concise without losing necessary meaning or evidence.
- Names, dates, figures, quotes, policies, and company claims are evidence-checked when required.
- Terminology, punctuation, capitalization, units, and bilingual conventions are stable.
- Line breaks do not distort names, numbers, or meaning.
- Placeholder, prompt, debug, or internal review text is absent from final output.

## 4. Visual system

- Grid, margins, anchors, spacing scale, and alignment match the Baseline or documented Candidate rules.
- Typefaces, weights, sizes, colors, and hierarchy are consistent.
- Imagery has intentional crop, resolution, color treatment, rights/evidence status, and captioning.
- Icons and illustrations share a coherent family and are not mixed by convenience.
- Decorative elements support hierarchy and do not compete with content.
- The composition still works at the real output ratio and target size.

## 5. Readability and no-drift verification

Use project-defined thresholds when available. If none exist, define a Candidate threshold appropriate to the medium and flag it for approval rather than inventing a permanent rule.

Inspect:

- text overflow, clipping, missing glyphs, unintended wrapping, hyphenation, orphaned lines, and line collisions;
- font substitution, weight mismatch, and illegible size at expected viewing distance;
- contrast against the rendered background, including images and gradients;
- safe zones, trim or bleed, display cutoffs, and physical obstruction zones;
- displacement of text boxes, images, labels, and anchors relative to the Baseline;
- page-to-page or zone-to-zone consistency for equivalent components;
- export-specific changes, including rasterization, scaling, missing fonts, and color shifts.

Any required readability or drift check that is not performed is `NOT_CHECKED`, not PASS.

## 6. Coverage examples

For slide decks, representative coverage can include cover, section divider, dense text, data, process, case study, image-led, and closing pages. For exhibitions, include entrance, long-view headline, close-reading panel, timeline, portrait, object caption, interactive, and wayfinding contexts as applicable.

Coverage is not complete because one hero page looks good. Every required family needs a representative Approved example and a passing real-context check.

## 7. Review output

Record:

- lifecycle and whether Baseline Continuation applies;
- structure, copy, and visual findings in separate groups;
- issue severity and the smallest safe fix;
- Baseline and Approved Seed IDs used;
- Candidate created and provenance;
- coverage, verify, and validate statuses;
- unresolved decisions or blockers.
